[Short pause]

CHAPTER 6 — THE VERDICT

On screen: “What we learned” — three team cards, jury line
Visual cue: Scene 6 — The Verdict

So. What did we actually take home from those two days?

[Short pause]

From Alpha, we learned this:

Many agents… create one clear picture.

They can see more than we can.

And earlier than we can.

Which means they can build a picture we would struggle to see ourselves.

[Short pause]

From Delta, we learned that agents can take care of the heavy, boring steps.

So people spend time on judgment — not assembly.

[Short pause]

From Bravo, we learned something we didn’t expect.

Multi-agent systems are strong for exploring, for ideas, for guidance.

But they’re a poor place to stop when the answer has to be legally correct.

[Short pause]

The jury highlighted Alpha’s demo.

But honestly — every team taught us something we needed to hear.

[Pause]

And then we realized something.

The bottleneck is no longer the code.

It is not the models.

It is clarity about what we wish to solve.

Because once we understood the problem…

building the agents was the easy part.

[Scroll]


CHAPTER 7 — THE WISDOM

On screen: Core insight quote
Visual cue: Scene 7 — The Wisdom

So what’s the actual lesson?

[Pause]

Agents are not just technology.

They behave more like a new kind of colleague.

Extremely capable.

Extremely fast.

And completely without judgment.

[Pause]

So they need boundaries.

Direction.

Orchestration.

And patience.

[Let it sit]

One agent is a tool.

You ask.

It answers.

But multiple agents with different roles and responsibilities?

[Pause]

That starts to look less like software…

and more like a workforce.

[Pause]

We thought the hard part would be the technology.

It wasn’t.

[Slow]

The hard part was deciding what should happen.

Who should do what.

What “good enough” actually means.

And when the system should stop… and ask for help.

[Pause]

In other words:

Execution is getting cheaper.

Clarity is becoming more valuable.

[Long pause]

[Gesture toward evolution row]

And what we saw, again and again, was the same pattern.

You start with a question.

You explore it.

You make something real.

Something people can react to.

And then… you refine.

[Short pause]

Not because the first answer was correct.

But because building something is now the fastest way to understand what you actually mean.

[Pause. Take a breath. Scroll to the final slide.]


CHAPTER 8 — THE FUTURE

On screen: “We walked in with a question. / We walked out with proof.”
Visual cue: Scene 8 — Stockholm dawn through glass, quiet office

We walked into Google’s office with a question.

[Pause]

We walked out with proof.

Proof that agents can see patterns humans miss.

Proof that ideas can move from thought… to working systems… in hours.

And proof that the distance between idea and software has collapsed.

[Pause]

Building is no longer the hard part.

[Short pause]

Understanding what to build is.

[Pause]

Because when creation becomes almost free…

bad assumptions scale faster too.

[Pause]

So maybe the real shift is not speed.

Maybe the real shift… is clarity.

[Slow]

The teams that learn fastest…

will not be the ones writing the most code.

They will be the ones discovering reality earlier.

Earlier feedback.

Earlier mistakes.

Earlier understanding.

[Pause]

Two days.

One room.

A few curious people.

That was enough to glimpse what comes next.

[Look across audience]

And now our responsibility at Skatteverket becomes even more important.

To build systems people can trust.

Systems that are fair. Transparent. Secure.

And sovereign by design.

[Final pause]

Because the future is not waiting for us anymore.

It is already here.

The question is whether we learn fast enough to shape it.

[Hold silence]

Thank you.
