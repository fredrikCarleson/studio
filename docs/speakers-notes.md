## Chapter 1 — Prologue

**On screen:** "Hackathon. Two days. Three teams. Fifteen brains."

[Visual Cue: Scene 1 — Stockholm sunrise, cinematic]

> Good morning.

[Pause]

> Last November, fifteen people from the Swedish Tax Agency walked into Google's Stockholm office.

> It smelled like coffee.
>
> And… a bit of expectation.

> We had two days. No preparation. One question.

> Can we get multiple AI agents to actually work together? Not just answer questions. Work. Like a team.

[Short Pause]

> We weren't there to build a product. We were there to understand.

> To feel where the edges are.

> Three teams. Three very different ideas. And what came out of those 48 hours changed how we think about AI at Skatteverket.

## Chapter 3 — The Swarm

**On screen:** Team Alpha — stats, agent cards, blueprint button
**This is the centerpiece. Build slowly. Use the sub-steps.**

[Visual Cue: Scene 3 — The Swarm background]

> In Sweden, more than ten thousand influencers make a living from brand deals and gifts.

> Most of them want to do the right thing. But tax rules are not always easy to interpret.

> The result? People slip into the hidden economy without even knowing it.

[Short Pause]

> Team Alpha asked: can we detect these risks automatically — before it becomes a problem for the influencer?

> They built five agents. One scrapes social media. Others value luxury items spotted in posts and videos. Risk profilers consolidate everything into a compliance report.

> What would normally take days of digging…
> showed up in minutes.

[Short Pause]

> But here's the part that made the jury lean forward.

> When the team showed how the same approach could be adapted for crime prevention — scanning for stolen goods, cross-referencing declared income with visible assets — that changed the conversation entirely.

[Press → to reveal the evidence card]

> And this is not theoretical.

[Point at the evidence card]

> This person went to jail for not paying taxes on gifts received as an influencer.

> Imagine if our swarm had nudged them months earlier. Proactive compliance instead of a prison sentence.

[Pause]

[Press B to open the blueprint]

> Let me ask our AI assistant to explain the architecture.

[The overlay opens. The MP3 narrates the five phases. Stand back. Point at the diagram as phases highlight.]

[After narration ends, press Esc]

> That. Built in 48 hours.

> Not by a vendor. By five people from a tax agency.

[Short Pause]

> Which is... not how government usually works.

[Scroll]

## Chapter 4 — The Pedagogy

**On screen:** "The Knowledge Split" — pull-quote, what vs how

[Visual Cue: Scene 4 — Knowledge split]

> Team Bravo built Skatti 2.0 — an AI trained on our official website to answer tax questions. 24/7. Never tired.

> At first, it seemed to work. Then they noticed something.

> The answers were confidently wrong.

[Pause — Let that land]

> Why? Our website explains what you need to do. But not how. And the web is full of "how" — some right, some very wrong. The agent couldn't tell the difference.

[Short Pause]

> For precise legal answers — not good enough. But for guidance and reasoning — genuinely useful.

> A promising colleague. Just one you occasionally have to correct.

[Pause. Slight smile.]

> "Skatti... you're actually making that up."

[Let the laugh settle. Scroll.]

## Chapter 6 — The Verdict

**On screen:** "What we learned" — three team cards, jury line

[Visual Cue: Scene 6 — The Verdict]

> So. What did we actually take home from those two days?

[Short Pause]

> From Alpha, we learned that many agents working together can build a picture no single human can hold. See more. See earlier.

> From Delta, we learned that agents can take care of the heavy, boring steps. So people spend time on judgment — not assembly.

> From Bravo, we learned something we didn't expect. Multi-agent systems are strong for exploring, for ideas, for guidance. But they're a poor place to stop when the bar is: legally correct, every time.

[Short Pause]

> The jury highlighted Alpha's demo. But honestly — every team taught us something we needed to hear.

[Short Pause]

> But there was something else.
>
> Something we didn't expect.

[Pause]

> The bottleneck was never the code.
>
> It was never the models.

[Short Pause]

> It was us.

[Pause]

> Or more specifically…

[Short Pause]

> how we think.

[Pause]

> Our ability to understand the problem.
>
> To frame it.
>
> To test it.
>
> To know what we were even trying to build.

[Short Pause]

> Because once that was clear…
> the rest happened surprisingly fast.

[Scroll]

## Chapter 7 — The Wisdom

**On screen:** Core insight quote

[Visual Cue: Scene 7 — The Wisdom]

> So what's the actual lesson?

[Pause. Read the quote slowly.]

> Agents are not just technology.

> They're teammates without judgment.

> They need boundaries. Orchestration. And patience.

[Long Pause — Let it echo]

> One agent is a tool. You ask. It answers.

> But multiple agents with roles and responsibilities? That becomes a workforce.

> We thought the hard part would be the technology.

[Short Pause]

> It wasn't.

[Pause]

> The hard part was deciding what should happen.
>
> Who does what.
>
> When something is "good enough".
>
> And when it needs to be questioned.

[Short Pause]

> In other words…
>
> not execution.

[Pause]

> Insight.

[Long Pause — Let it echo]

[Gesture at the evolution row on screen]

> We're on a journey.

> And what we saw, again and again, was a simple loop.

[Short Pause]

> You start with a question.
>
> You explore it.
>
> You make something real.
>
> Something you can react to.
>
> And then you refine it.

[Pause]

> Not because it's perfect.
>
> But because now you can react to it.

[Pause. Take a breath. Scroll to the final slide.]
